# 29-DoF HoST A6 V19 evaluation report

Date: 2026-10-16

## Scope

V19 tests the scene-isolated residual formulation requested after the flat
regression audit. The selected V38 standing policy and its observation
normalizer are frozen. A separate PPO residual may alter actions only while an
obstacle scene is active, the plate has not been escaped, and motors are on.
Flat scenes and post-escape behavior use the exact frozen V38 action.

This is an adapter-scale diagnostic at action scale `0.5723877549`. It is not
the complete paper method and is not a formal action-scale-`0.25` result.

## Why flat behavior changed in earlier A6 runs

Earlier acquisition runs updated the standing actor itself while changing its
action scale, observation distribution, reset distribution, and reward. That
allowed obstacle learning to overwrite the already useful flat-standing
controller. The issue was therefore not that V38 lacked flat standing torque;
it was interference between the standing policy and the A6 adaptation path.

Directly remapping the V38 action from its source scale to `0.25` is not a
safe substitute. The required gain saturates about `68%` of actions, and the
resulting policy fails. V19 instead preserves the base policy and isolates all
learned obstacle corrections behind a hard scene gate.

- Residual scale: `0.25`
- Residual PPO: std `0.06`, learning rate `1e-5`, two epochs
- Episode length: 1,150 control steps
- Training resets: obstacle scenes only, guided/free plate split equally
- Gate: `(scene > 0) and not escaped and motor-on`
- Flat and post-escape contract: applied residual is exactly zero
- G+ positive task reward alpha: fixed at one
- G+ includes the plate-center-separation diagnostic reward

Both 64-environment, 1,150-step preflights passed with an explicit flat,
guided-plate, and free-plate reset mix. They verified a zero initial residual,
exactly zero applied residual in flat scenes, finite rewards, gradients and
normalizer statistics, and no NaN/Inf. The final flat audits also recorded an
applied-residual maximum of exactly zero.

## Selection and conclusion

- Retain G- update 200 only as a diagnostic residual checkpoint.
- Select no trained G+ residual; update 0 is better than every trained point.
- Do not replace the repository's selected V38 standing model.
- Do not run 2,048-environment evaluation or obstacle video for V19.

V19 validates the correct software architecture for protecting flat standing,
but it does not solve A6. Primary success remains `0/256` for both groups at
every checkpoint, vertical escape remains `0-3%`, and the experiment is still
far from the formal `0.25` action scale. The remaining problem is not ordinary
flat retention; it is learning a low-reset get-up and obstacle-recovery policy
without saturating or overwriting the frozen standing controller.

A follow-up should change supervision or state coverage rather than repeat
PPO reward tuning. Suitable directions are an explicit get-up/recovery
teacher, obstacle-conditioned residual state, or trajectory-level action
distillation with a no-change constraint on the frozen base distribution.
